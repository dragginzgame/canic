# Canic 0.110 Design: Fleet Runtime Contraction

## Final Feature Boundary — accepted 2026-09-25

The maintainer targets `.42` as the final planned 0.110 feature release and
selects blob service extraction as the next major slice. This amendment owns
the current scope and supersedes the earlier requirement to implement every
B3/B4 contraction before closeout. It does not claim those cuts are complete.

- B1 is accepted and B2 is complete with its specifically accepted cold-query
  tradeoffs and retained measurements.
- B3 stops without further record/codec restructuring. This is a priority
  decision, not a new measurement proving no residual benefit.
- Unimplemented B4 pruning is deferred and unscheduled. B5 must record the
  remaining Canic-owned generated surfaces and owners as explicit residuals;
  their presence alone no longer blocks this amended contraction scope.
- B5 final-source qualification remains required: canonical/fixture reserves,
  interfaces, determinism, implemented behavior and same-release operational
  safety. A budget deficit or confirmed correctness/security/recovery defect
  cannot be reclassified as optional pruning.
- The complete `.42` batch remains governed by its direct qualification and
  release flow. Necessary defect corrections may follow on 0.110; `.42` is a
  feature target, not a promise to leave a discovered regression unfixed.
- The exact 0.110 closeout audit must still be requested by the human and its
  verdict accepted before 0.111 implementation. This planning amendment is
  neither that audit request nor acceptance.

The line already exceeds the twelve-release guideline. Consolidate final
qualification and any confirmed corrections instead of allocating releases to
more speculative contraction slices. The current handoff and tracker own
progress; historical checkpoints below retain their dated evidence only.

## RF2: receipt-safe operator ICP conversion

RF2 was preserved outside .18 on 2026-09-15. CANIC-172's retained original
withdrawal blocker brings it into the open .24 batch. The host now implements
review, payment transport, authenticated receipt acquisition, once-only credit
admission and operator CLI. The current handoff distinguishes production-Ledger
PocketIC evidence, native original-withdrawal recovery and live staging limits.

The maintainer continued the accepted recovery sequence after publishing
0.110.17 on 2026-09-15. RF2 precedes RF3's complete live recovery forecasts and
the remaining B1 attribution. This is an in-repository implementation contract,
not authority to spend ICP, deploy, publish or change a sibling repository.
The existing host funding workflow and journal own RF2; runtime canisters do
not gain a second funding owner or a new stable store.

### Reviewed intent and effect ownership

An explicit funding review binds the original Fleet operation and plan,
the funding pause/review digest, selected network/trust anchor, signer Principal,
ICP Ledger, CMC, Cycles Ledger and exact source/destination accounts. The first
delivery uses the existing operator's default accounts. The ICP destination is
the CMC account with the selected operator's Principal-derived subaccount.
Different account modes are outside this batch.

The review may describe supplementary Root/estate funding or the original first
Fund intent retained by the .23 admission defect. The latter binds the original
action and plan without introducing another withdrawal. The original timestamp,
source seals and starting balances remain unchanged; an Intent or a subsequent
insufficient-funds reply is never proof of nonpayment. Fresh operations check
maximum debit before journalling, with an advisory ICP quote against the selected
plan. Pre-build readiness assessment is separate follow-up work.


The review authorizes one exact ICP amount in e8s plus its exact transfer fee.
It retains the complete transfer arguments, including a nonzero fixed
`created_at_time` and the protocol's MINT memo. A separate, domain-separated
32-byte deposit memo binds the conversion to its original operation and review.
The CMC notification arguments are fixed before submission too. Conversion-rate
samples explain the quote but never establish an actual credit or authorize a
second payment when the realized credit is insufficient.

Model owns the retained intent/receipt invariants; ops constructs records,
encodes requests and performs individual calls; pure policy admits evidence and
computes exact amounts; workflow persists transitions and orchestrates retries.
CLI adds review/apply/status projections through the existing Fleet owner.
Do not implement this by invoking the existing ICP CLI mint command, which
discards receipt identifiers and does not fix transfer time or deposit memo.

### Same-operation interruption contract

| Retained state | Permitted next work | Forbidden inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reviewed, not approved | Refresh an unapproved quote or display its digest | Review alone never permits an ICP transfer |
| Approved, transfer intent durable | Submit the exact ICP transfer; retain success or duplicate block identity | A lost reply never permits a new timestamp, amount or second transfer |
| Transfer unresolved | Reconcile the exact transfer or resume its identical request while admissible | Expired duplicate history, a balance decrease or an unverified candidate block is not proof of non-execution |
| ICP block bound | Validate the transaction and persist exact CMC notification intent before notification | A block number alone does not bind amount, source, destination, fee, memo or time |
| Notification unresolved | Retry notification for the same ICP block and fixed arguments; retain Processing/refund/terminal evidence | Never fund a second conversion to escape a pending or purged notification |
| Mint result retained | Authenticate and validate the referenced Cycles Ledger deposit block | Cached notification arguments or the returned account balance are not destination/credit proof |
| Receipt admitted | Record one net credit, then resume the original approved withdrawal or separately approved supplementary funding | The credit neither approves a withdrawal nor changes original starting balances |
| Complete | Return retained receipts and accounting without effects | Replay never transfers, notifies or withdraws again |

Use the existing operation lock and durable-write boundary. A process restart
reconstructs the exact selected signer, intent and receipt chain. Missing,
inconsistent or unavailable evidence gives a typed retained recovery state.
The current journal retains one transfer reply and one exact notification intent
with its latest reply. These are bounded observations, not a call-attempt history.
A known transfer block cannot be replaced by a different block or a later error;
mint/refund replies cannot be replaced by conflicting outcomes. Notification bytes
must match the original intent and retained ICP block. These local observations
alone cannot credit the account, complete the conversion or approve withdrawal.
Expired transfer/notification history requires bounded receipt discovery and
authenticated evidence, or an explicit unresolved review; it never authorizes
a replacement payment. Do not promise effect-free replay for an unresolved
operation or completion merely because a balance became sufficient.

### Receipt admission and conservation

Retain the ICP transaction block and exact Cycles Ledger deposit block, including
their respective ledger identities. The verified ICP transaction must match all
reviewed transfer fields. The verified Cycles Ledger transaction must be a mint
to the exact operator account with the operation-bound deposit memo and exact
amount/fee fields. Decode external natural-number identifiers losslessly and
reject amounts that cannot fit Canic's bounded accounting types.

Upstream CMC notification history is keyed by ICP block and may return an earlier
result without reapplying the newly supplied destination/memo. Therefore a cached
success is only a locator until the deposit transaction is verified. Its historical
`balance` is diagnostic data, not a current balance or a credit amount.

Record gross minted cycles, the actual deposit fee and net operator credit as
separate quantities. Require `gross = net + deposit_fee` with checked arithmetic.
Add only the net credit, once, to the existing operation's operator-cycle equation;
the later withdrawal debit and fee retain their existing accounting. Never deduct
the deposit fee again from that net credit or overwrite initial operator/estate
balances. Admission rejects reused ICP/deposit identities, wrong network/account/
memo, conflicting amounts, missing fees and arithmetic overflow. Serialized local
fields alone are not authenticated Ledger evidence.

ICP has its own exact equation: reviewed transfer amount plus transfer fee,
any verified refund, and any bounded observed refund/processing debit. A refund
produces no mint credit. A refund without an attributable block remains explicit
unresolved or bounded terminal evidence; no invented refund amount or cycle value
may close it. Foreign account activity is never attributed to this conversion.

### Certified deposit adapter

The host receipt ops authenticates the Cycles Ledger certificate with the existing
ic-agent verifier, including its delegation/canister-range and timestamp policy.
The reviewed network identity is SHA-256 over
`canic:operator-mint:network-root:v1` followed by NUL and the selected DER root key.
The reader obtains that trust anchor from selected network configuration, never
from the receipt response. Gateway URLs do not define separate IC networks.
The Agent's root configuration must stay fixed during verification.

The certificate's exact Ledger `certified_data` must match the witness digest.
The witness supplies `last_block_hash` and unsigned-LEB128 `last_block_index`.
A bounded descending chain must connect that tip to the retained deposit index;
each link uses upstream ICRC3 hashing. The block projection matches the reviewed
Ledger's wider `Value` contract, including its `Nat64` alternative, rather than
assuming `ICRC3Value` wire equality. Hashing borrows values without cloning blocks.
The caller supplies certificate bytes, block count, value bytes/nodes and nesting
budgets, and must separately bound transport response decoding. Old deposits
require correspondingly longer chains and may exceed that budget; there is no
constant-cost historical proof claim. Oversized signed integers reject before the
upstream hasher's signed-128-bit assumption can panic. There is no balance-delta
fallback when the evidence is incomplete or exceeds the selected budget.

The maintained deposit schema has a `mint` transaction with the operator's
single-element default-account array, exact memo, positive net `amt` and actual
transaction `fee`. The block-level effective fee must be zero; it is not the
deposit fee. Extra transaction fields or unsupported account/schema shapes reject.
Checked net plus fee must equal the retained CMC gross amount. Historical balance
is ignored. Only the verifier constructs the resulting deposit evidence, which
retains its exact intent and amounts and cannot be deserialized. This evidence
cannot establish the ICP debit or admit credit alone.
The execution workflow authenticates both receipts before once-only journal
admission. CBOR certificates use an owned bounded hash-tree decoder, including
pruned nodes; signature, delegation, canister-range and timestamp checks remain
in the existing Agent verifier.

### ICP replicated receipt adapter

The reviewed ICP Ledger certifies the last encoded block hash, without a certified
height. Its ordinary query's reported tip/first index is insufficient authority
for numbering a short chain. Canic instead prepares a replicated
`query_encoded_blocks` call for exactly one selected index. The signed request ID
binds its sender, Ledger, method, argument, expiry and nonce. Only a valid
certificate containing `request_status/<that ID>/status = replied` and that
request's reply may establish the returned block. The selected network root and
operator identity must match the retained intent before preparation and verification.

This uses the IC's supported
[replicated-query path](https://docs.internetcomputer.org/guides/security/data-integrity-and-authenticity/).
It is a prepared receipt observation, not a transfer or a new funding owner.
The acquisition adapter submits that signed observation and polls its exact
request status within a bounded call timeout. A restart may repeat this read;
it never changes or substitutes the original transfer request.

The Ledger reply must contain exactly the selected local block within its stated
chain length, or authorize one archive range with exactly that index and length
one, no local blocks, and the fixed `get_encoded_blocks` callback. Anonymous,
management and Ledger-self callbacks reject. The Ledger's first local index is
unspecified when it returns no local blocks and is not used for archive authority.

Only a verified Ledger reply constructs the private, non-deserializable archive
authorization. Ops can then sign a one-block replicated read for that archive
under the same reviewed network and operator. The archive's independently
certified request-status reply must authenticate the archive's canister/subnet
range, its exact request ID, and one original encoded transfer. It cannot redirect
again. Missing/extra blocks and typed archive errors remain unresolved; callback
authorization alone proves no debit and grants no credit. Durable restoration
must reverify retained evidence before reconstructing this authority; it cannot
deserialize an authorization or trust an unauthenticated cached callback.

Certificate bytes, reply bytes, CBOR depth and Candid decoding/skipping work have
explicit reader bounds. Non-replied requests and unavailable evidence remain
unresolved, with no inference about whether the original payment executed.

The verifier uses the original encoded protobuf block. The decoded Candid block
view can substitute the block timestamp for an absent transfer timestamp, and
upstream decoding can supply a default transfer fee. Neither is sufficient proof
of the exact reviewed request. The maintained projection requires the canonical
direct-send fields, full account identifiers, explicit amount/fee/memo/time and
exact intent equality. Canonical re-encoding rejects unknown fields, extensions,
duplicates and unsupported encodings. It does not reconstruct historical Ledger
defaults. The private verified ICP result contains its exact intent, index and
encoded-block hash; it cannot be deserialized or independently credit cycles.

### Qualification and implementation order

1. Define named intent, transfer/notification outcome, receipt and accounting
   records and pure checked admission. Exercise missing/conflicting bindings,
   duplicate credit, gross/net confusion, overflow and byte-stable request identity.
2. Integrate durable review/approval and intent-before-effect transitions into
   the existing host journal. Keep original plan/balances intact; fail before
   effects on changed signer/network/account/digest. Add truthful CLI diagnostics.
3. Add exact ICP transfer, CMC notification and authenticated Ledger receipt
   adapters. Prove the supported block/certificate and bounded discovery paths;
   unknown schemas or incomplete evidence stay unresolved. No plain balance-delta
   or unchecked query-response fallback is admissible.
4. Use PocketIC to qualify response loss after transfer, after notification and
   before local credit persistence; duplicate transfer replies, Processing,
   refunds, exhausted recovery evidence, restart, separate withdrawal approval,
   same retained child recovery, terminal conservation and immediate replay.
   Native fixtures alone do not qualify IC effects or deployed CMC behavior.

All four steps, direct propagation and cleanup form RF2's completion boundary.
The first step does not make RF2 push-ready. Existing already-funded recovery
remains usable and must not depend on new conversion or descendant telemetry.

Toko's 2026-09-15 CANIC-172 follow-up sharpens the same required evidence:
preserve request bytes/time after reply loss and restart; reject cached CMC
results whose actual deposit memo/account differs; interrupt both before and
after credit persistence without changing starting balances or crediting twice;
reject signer/network drift before calls; and retain Processing, unattributed
refund and purged-history outcomes without inventing credit. Native cases cover
review/approval persistence, fencing, invalid evidence and
original-withdrawal recovery. The production-Ledger PocketIC journey covers lost
transfer and notification replies, interruption before credit persistence, actual
fees, authenticated once-only credit and conversion replay. Its subsequent Fleet
funding uses a host model; a complete live Toko Fleet recovery is not established.

## Promoted downstream operator batches

On 2026-09-13 the maintainer explicitly brings CANIC-010, 008, 002 and 017

### OP1: operator Component lifecycle (CANIC-010)

The host owns a reviewed, durable invocation of Root's existing
`ProvisionComponent` command and operation-status lane. The CLI selects a
terminal Fleet, exact Root, admitted Component Spec and a local operation name.
Planning is effect-free. Applying the exact review digest persists submission
intent before the command and observes the original operation after any lost
response. Root remains the allocation, installation, Directory and activation
owner. Initial Component Group provisioning keeps its existing Fleet owner.

Authority binds environment/network, App/Fleet, Coordinator/Root/subnet,
installed release/module/Candid, controller, spec hash and selected role.
Changing the requested name, spec, placement or release cannot retarget an
issued operation. Terminal replay returns the original Component/Principal
without another command. Status reports incomplete work honestly and preserves
typed Root failures. Each apply reserves at most one submission and then polls status within the
selected wait bound. Replaying an incomplete existing operation resumes Root's
heap driver after restoration; Root coalesces concurrent scheduling for its
operation ID. No uncertain work is abandoned. A pending operation remains resumable under the same
review; a second operation is an explicit new local name.

Qualification includes pure authority rejection and crash-boundary persistence,
an actual PocketIC Root journey with lost response/restart, wrong caller,
capacity rejection and effect-free terminal replay. The public packaged CLI
must suffice for the application-neutral consumer fixture.

### OP2: frontend handoff (CANIC-008)

A read-only host exporter emits a bounded schema-1 frontend manifest from
verified terminal Fleet authority. It binds App/Fleet/network, release identity,
exact exported role Principals, Candid and generated binding hashes, endpoint,
explicit local trust, Internet Identity origin/Principal and optional externally
managed static-asset origin/Principal. A digest covers fields and referenced
artifacts. Unknown/nonterminal roles and stale bindings reject. Credentials,
controller proofs and mutation tokens never enter the output.

Current Fleet installation admission owns user Principals; the former App
whitelist is removed. Selected-environment identity-origin input therefore binds
the protected Fleet review before installation, rather than recompiling an
obsolete whitelist. Export rejects a different or absent origin binding for a
nonempty admission policy. No Principal list enters the browser manifest.

Frontend build/asset ownership remains external. Canic supplies the supported
handoff and one independent fixture, not an application-specific renderer or
asset-canister controller. Qualification covers current/stale identities,
tampered artifacts, local trust isolation, generated browser bindings and one
authenticated App/Component call using the maintained authorization contract.

### OP3: host-first Fleet observatory (CANIC-002)

The host projects bounded existing protected status and terminal inventory into
passive views of identity, placement, admission/release, funding, estate counts,
incomplete operations and typed blockers. Every observation states provenance
and freshness; failure of one role cannot manufacture success or erase other
role observations. A supported downstream adapter consumes this view. HTML,
CSS and application sections stay outside canonical runtime roles. There is no
new every-role endpoint, renderer, polling canister or public internal record.

Qualification includes truthful partial results, stale/unknown state, independent
role failures, response/collection bounds and measured host/artifact cost. The
existing public metrics contract remains distinct from exact protected inventory.

### OP4: persistent multi-subnet development Fleet (CANIC-017)

Public host developer tooling owns a bounded PocketIC process/state directory,
Coordinator plus at least two Roots/Stores, declared Components and a stable
browser gateway. It uses maintained artifact and Fleet convergence owners and
public testing primitives rather than depending on internal fixture crates.
Discovery reuses the exact public routing/trust handoff. Status, restart, time
advance, reset and shutdown identify one invocation-owned local environment;
cleanup never sweeps other processes or state directories.

Qualification includes packaged downstream startup, two-Root placement and
cross-subnet calls, browser discovery, restart/lost responses, deterministic time,
bounded cache/resources and clean shutdown. The product explicitly distinguishes
local simulation from mainnet charging, latency, consensus and Registry fidelity.
The CANIC-141 scalar-fee restriction must remain truthful: simulated free
creation does not authorize using one scalar fee across paid mainnet subnets.

All four batches retain same-operation recovery, exact release binding and
cycle conservation. Runtime expansion requires concrete measurement and a
bounded contract; host-only orchestration is preferred where it satisfies the
requested outcome.

## Why This Line Changed

The earlier tracked 0.110 revision put build acceleration, operator inventory,
status cleanup and stateful retirement ahead of the strongest measured Wasm
work. It also measured defined functions without requiring final function
headroom. That structure could spend the reserve it was supposed to create.

The restored direction follows the actual evidence:

1. Binaryen has already produced its material general-purpose reduction;
2. the remaining downstream pressure is both code-section size and the exact
   local-function quantity enforced by the replica validator;
3. shallow post-`v0.109.28` attribution found large overlapping
   Serde/Ciborium reachability around Canic stable records; and
4. global storage registration can make role-inapplicable records, codecs and
   recovery work reachable in otherwise small actors; and
5. non-storage macro expansions can retain all-role endpoints, Candid roots,
   providers, timers and control-plane dispatch even when the consuming actor
   does not expose those capabilities; and
6. downstream artifacts can expose combined pressure, but Canic must qualify
   its own contraction from repository-owned roles and capability fixtures.

The roughly 725 KiB Canic-associated Ciborium total is routing evidence, not a
savings forecast. Activation, authorization and other symbol families overlap,
and shared machinery may remain after one family is removed. Controlled
ablation builds and fresh marginal measurements therefore own every
implementation decision.

## Decision

The contraction work follows these decisions, alongside the separately accepted
operator/recovery batches. The 2026-09-25 amendment stops further record/codec
work and defers remaining generated-surface pruning:

1. Every retained runtime capability has one owner, one compiled projection
   and one measured inclusive and marginal cost under a named immutable build.
   Shared artifact costs are never treated as additive.
2. Every consuming actor generates an exact role/capability-specific runtime
   graph. Retain the implemented direct initialization, restore and recovery
   cuts and inventory any residual dispatch under the B4 deferral; do not
   reintroduce the removed executable TLS callback registry.
3. Stable TLS is lazy. Defining a stable domain in `canic-core` does not make
   its record and serializer reachable in every role.
4. Further capability-owned activation/authorization record splits are stopped;
   retained aggregate records are explicit residuals, not claimed removed.
5. A stable codec changes only after current marginal evidence justifies it.
6. Exported endpoints remain thin marshalling/authentication/delegation
   boundaries; repeated work is shared only when optimized artifact evidence
   proves that LLVM did not recreate the duplication.
7. Remaining unused command, metric, provisioning, configuration, diagnostic
   and recovery implementations receive explicit B5 residual dispositions.
8. Each runtime batch ends with a fresh byte/function report and an explicit
   stop, continue or retarget decision.
9. Existing security, Candid, status, recovery and operator semantics remain
   intact except for the pre-1.0 stable-codec hard cut expressly selected by
   measured evidence.
10. Importing or splitting a Rust macro receives no contraction credit by
    itself. The accepted evidence is what each expansion references and what
    the final optimized role artifact proves absent.
11. Downstream artifacts are optional pressure observations, not release gates,
    savings-attribution authorities or Canic production dependencies.

B1 also records the dated authoritative network limits for code-section bytes,
total module bytes and defined functions. The acceptance measurement uses a
repository-owned counter frozen to the IC replica validator source; it does not
substitute `ic-wasm`'s imports-plus-definitions total. The optimizer's defined-
function metric remains a separately produced cross-check.
Completion is defined against the final artifact:

The frozen interpretation follows the
[IC WebAssembly module requirements](https://docs.internetcomputer.org/references/ic-interface-spec/canister-interface/),
which state the function limit in terms of functions declared by the module.

```text
code_headroom =
    network_code_section_limit - final_code_section_bytes

function_headroom =
    network_defined_function_limit - final_replica_limited_function_count

required_function_reserve =
    ceil(network_defined_function_limit * 5 / 100)

required_code_reserve =
    ceil(network_code_section_limit * 5 / 100)
```

`final_replica_limited_function_count` is the exact local-function quantity
counted by the frozen replica validator. At DFINITY `ic` commit
`2f8dc21e2e5c37a4cae7f65d2a4230ac8f143e5a`,
`rs/embedders/src/wasm_utils/validation.rs` filters the module function arena
with `is_local()` before applying `max_functions`; imports are validated
separately and are not included. `rs/config/src/embedders.rs` sets that default
to 50,000. B1 records the repository counter source and executable hashes,
frozen IC commit, structural-validator version and result, and rejects a
disagreement with the independently emitted optimizer defined-function count.

The worst qualified artifact must satisfy all independent gates:

```text
code_headroom >= required_code_reserve
function_headroom >= required_function_reserve
final_total_module_bytes < network_total_module_limit
```

Under the retained 10 MiB code-section limit, the binding 5% reserve is
512 KiB. A 1 MiB code-section reserve is preferred. Reduction from a failing
baseline is useful attribution but never completion evidence. If a network
limit or toolchain changes, the baseline and accepted limits must be frozen
again.

Representative workloads receive one explicit maximum 1% instruction
regression allowance. B1 may select a smaller allowance; later batches cannot
silently expand it.

### Positional Codec Hard Cut

Only a record family that remains materially attributable after the preceding
remeasurement may replace named-map CBOR. Each selected stable wire type is
separate from its domain record and public DTO and has:

- a distinct magic/prefix that legacy bytes cannot match accidentally;
- codec version 1 separate from logical record-schema version 1;
- positional fields and numeric enum discriminants;
- fixed endianness for multibyte values;
- one canonical optional-field representation;
- exact maximum lengths checked before allocation;
- an explicit maximum encoded size;
- rejection of trailing bytes; and
- independently retained golden vectors not generated by the tested encoder.

No predecessor decoder, compatibility alias or fallback remains. The host and
actor lifecycle reject an attempted predecessor upgrade before interpreting
stable state or performing another effect. Direct qualification proves that
the rejected upgrade leaves code, stable state, authority and workflow effects
unchanged, and that only the authorized reinstall path can cross the hard cut.

B1 must inventory every state domain deliberately destroyed by the hard cut
and name how fresh Root, authentication, topology and application authority is
reconstructed after reinstall. Missing reconstruction authority blocks the
codec change.

### Reinstall-Only Release Enforcement

The hard cut is executable release policy, not narrative guidance. Governed
0.110 release metadata records a machine-readable reinstall-only transition
mode. Host planning and release qualification must reject an ordinary upgrade,
adoption or mixed-version transition for an already installed stateful Fleet
before issuing any install, stop, controller, funding or other platform effect.

Only the separately authorized reinstall path may cross the release boundary.
It must first prove the reviewed destroyed-state inventory, reconstruction
authority, controller authority and cycle-conservation plan. Direct negative
qualification records the typed pre-effect rejection and unchanged module,
stable state, controller, cycle and workflow observations. No generic
`upgrade` command, mode inference or fallback may silently turn the hard cut
into an upgrade attempt.
