## Why This Line Changed

Canic already proves two independent properties:

1. one Fleet can contain multiple Roots on distinct physical Subnets; and
2. multiple Fleets can remain authority-isolated even when their Roots share a
   physical Subnet.

The former 0.110 estate design tried to add indexed accounting, stateful
retirement, high-throughput creation/reset, reserve Fleets, cross-Fleet
transfer, funding automation and 10/100/1,000 qualification together. 0.109
showed why that is unsafe: each additional autonomous phase multiplies exact
retry, observation, interruption and downstream adoption paths.

This line therefore adds only the smallest useful multi-Fleet asset operation
after runtime contraction and exact stateful-transition safety are already
proven.

## Decision

0.112 makes these decisions:

1. Every Fleet Subnet Root owns one indexed ledger of physical Canisters under
   its current controller and Fleet authority.
2. Total owned estate and Ready reserve are separate values. Claiming a Ready
   asset does not automatically create a replacement merely to restore the
   reserve.
3. A reserve Fleet is an ordinary Fleet with explicit Roots and empty
   application topology. It gains no special role or authority.
4. A physical asset may transfer only between exact Roots on the same observed
   physical Subnet.
5. The first maintained transfer cardinality is one asset per operation.
6. Transfer changes physical lifecycle authority only. The destination assigns
   fresh Fleet, Component and operation identity; no application state, Wasm or
   logical membership crosses.
7. The source proves the asset empty, module-free, unbound and terminal. If its
   most recent workload required application retirement, the exact qualified
   receipt or permanent forced-removal tombstone is also mandatory.
8. The destination persists capacity and exact-Principal reservation before
   controller mutation.
9. Controller convergence passes through one bounded co-control state and ends
   with the destination Root as sole controller.
10. Both Roots retain terminal receipts. Response loss re-observes and resumes
    the same transfer; it never selects another asset or destination.
11. Funding separates Root operating cycles, the estate Cycles Ledger budget,
    retained asset balances and claim top-ups.
12. The line inherits 0.110 code, build, operator and validation budgets.

## Non-Goals

0.112 does not:

- create or transfer a Root, Coordinator or Wasm Store;
- move a Canister between physical Subnets;
- transfer application data, stable memory, Wasm or logical identity;
- infer ownership from controller status alone;
- add rolling or mixed-version operation;
- add automatic high-throughput creation/reset lanes;
- transfer several assets under one operation;
- promise 1,000-canister scale;
- add an on-canister Observatory, renderer or Fleet projection protocol;
- make Toko Miner or IcyDB a production dependency; or
- add a second Fleet convergence owner beside Fleet Ensure and the exact
  Root-local transfer records.

## Downstream Steering Boundary

Toko Miner steers the required operator and application shape:

- real multi-Subnet managed topology;
- exact no-effect plan and reviewed-digest apply;
- visible cycle conservation and separate funding classes;
- stateful retirement before destructive reuse;
- fail-closed current authority without downstream state synthesis; and
- immediate effect-free replay after terminal convergence.

Canic proves the shape through a repository-owned two-Fleet fixture. Toko may
confirm the public contracts read-only after publication. Its mutable topology,
canisters, cycles and source are never Canic test authority.

## Authority Map

| Concern | Sole authority |
| --- | --- |
| Fleet identity and membership | exact Coordinator Registry authority |
| Root identity and placement | Fleet Registry plus live management routing evidence |
| physical asset ownership | source or destination Root indexed estate record plus live controller evidence |
| application retirement | 0.110 role-owned receipt bound to the immutable removal operation |
| transfer intent | source and destination Root records under one operation identity |
| destination capacity | destination Root reservation index |
| controller mutation | reviewed host plan invoking IC management under both Root receipts |
| transfer completion | both terminal Root receipts plus sole-controller observation |
| operator debit | content-addressed Fleet Ensure plan and conservation report |

The host composes evidence but is not an authority cache. A local ICP identity
may sign reviewed actions; its name or Principal never becomes implicit asset
authority.

## Indexed Estate Contract

Each Root maintains bounded counters and indexes for:

- total owned physical assets;
- Ready assets;
- claimed/installed assets;
- draining or recycling assets;
- failed or uncertain assets;
- export-reserved assets;
- import-reserved assets; and
- terminal transferred-out tombstones.

Hot decisions use counters and keyed indexes, not complete stable-memory scans.
Every counter change is coupled atomically to the authoritative row change.
Corrupt counts, duplicate Principal ownership, impossible state combinations or
missing reverse indexes fail closed and remain diagnosable.

The initial policy distinguishes:

- `owned_target`: desired total reusable assets;
- `owned_maximum`: hard first-excess bound;
- `ready_reserve`: assets protected from ordinary claims;
- `standby_cycles`: minimum retained balance for an empty asset; and
- `claim_top_up_cycles`: one exact top-up before installation when needed.

Changing a target never destroys an asset, cancels an issued effect or creates
work above the reviewed maximum.

## Reserve Fleet

A reserve Fleet contains ordinary Canic infrastructure:

```text
one Coordinator
  + one Root per selected physical Subnet
  + one sibling Store per Root
  + zero application Components
```

Its Registry, funding, admission and Root authority remain ordinary Fleet
contracts. Empty topology means it has no application release or membership
authority. A reserve Fleet may hold empty reusable assets and transfer them to
another Fleet only through the exact protocol below.

## Transfer Eligibility

Before either Root accepts transfer intent, evidence must prove:

- source and destination Fleet, Root and physical Subnet identity;
- the same current physical Subnet for both Roots and the asset;
- source sole control of the asset;
- no installed module;
- no application binding or nonterminal operation;
- no outstanding retirement requirement: a never-bound asset needs no
  application receipt, while the most recent opted-in stateful binding needs
  exact qualified retirement or a separately authorized permanent
  unqualified-removal tombstone;
- balance at or above the accepted standby minimum;
- destination capacity below its maximum;
- no other export/import reservation for the Principal; and
- sufficient bounded management-call and controller authority.

Wrong, missing, stale or contradictory evidence rejects before effects.

## Single-Asset Transfer Protocol

One operation proceeds:

1. destination persists capacity and exact-Principal import reservations;
2. source persists the matching export intent and terminal source eligibility;
3. host freezes one content-addressed plan binding both receipts, live
   management evidence, controller sequence and zero application authority;
4. source adds the destination Root as controller while retaining itself;
5. both Roots re-observe the exact co-control set;
6. destination rechecks no module, Subnet, balance and reservation authority;
7. source removes itself from the controller set;
8. destination re-observes sole control and commits the asset under fresh
   destination logical identity; and
9. source observes loss of control and commits an immutable transferred-out
   tombstone.

Before co-control, exact cancellation may release both reservations if no
controller effect was issued. After co-control, recovery is forward-only to
the named destination. Uncertainty retains both reservations and permits only
observation or exact retry.

## Funding And Conservation

The plan reports separately:

- source Root operating burn;
- destination Root operating burn;
- estate Cycles Ledger debit;
- asset native balance before and after transfer;
- claim top-up, if a later destination claim requires it;
- management-call uncertainty margin; and
- maximum operator debit.

Transfer itself neither creates an asset nor sweeps excess cycles. High balance
is informational evidence. Any later claim top-up is a distinct exact
destination operation and cannot be hidden inside controller transfer.

No automatic external funding extension is implied. A replay-safe estate
budget replenishment remains an unnumbered idea until separately earned.

## Failure And Recovery

The design proves interruption:

1. before either reservation;
2. after one reservation;
3. after both reservations and before controller mutation;
4. after controller request with response loss;
5. in co-control;
6. after destination sole control but before destination commit;
7. after destination commit but before source tombstone; and
8. after terminal receipts but before host observation.

Every phase has one permitted next action. A restart reads Root records and
live management evidence; it does not trust host prose or a stale cached
snapshot. Terminal replay produces no controller, funding or state mutation.

## Operator Surface

Fleet Ensure remains the common plan/apply boundary. The operator sees:

- source and destination network/Fleet/Root/Subnet identity;
- exact asset Principal and current/final controller sets;
- retirement disposition;
- operation and plan digest;
- reservations and current phase;
- each cycle class and maximum debit;
- blocker and sole next action; and
- terminal receipts and effect-free replay result.

The CLI does not expose one command per internal phase. Status is bounded and
authorization occurs before protected estate or transfer records are read.

## Size And Validation Discipline

Every estate/transfer type and command/status variant is capability-attributed.
Roles without estate ownership compile none of the Root estate workflow. The
endpoint-heavy managed fixture must not lose 0.110 headroom.

Validation uses pure transition tables and host fixtures for the complete
interruption matrix. PocketIC retains only:

- indexed estate create/claim/restart behavior;
- one same-Subnet transfer with response loss; and
- one two-Fleet/two-Subnet isolation and terminal replay journey.

The two-Fleet fixture has a Root for each Fleet on each of two Subnets. It
transfers one empty asset between the co-located Roots on one Subnet and proves
that neither Fleet's other Root, Store, Registry, pool or application authority
changes. No cross-Subnet transfer is attempted or implied.

The complete line remains within inherited 0.110 time/RSS/process and size
budgets. A scale claim larger than the fixture requires separate measured
evidence and promotion.

## Completion Criteria

0.112 is complete only when:

1. estate hot decisions use bounded indexes and counters without full scans;
2. owned target, maximum and Ready reserve remain distinct;
3. a reserve Fleet is ordinary infrastructure with empty application topology;
4. only an empty, unbound same-Subnet asset with every applicable retirement
   requirement satisfied may transfer;
5. destination capacity is reserved before controller mutation;
6. one transfer operation owns exactly one asset and destination;
7. co-control recovery moves forward without alternate selection;
8. destination assigns fresh logical identity and receives no source
   application authority;
9. both Roots retain terminal receipts and replay is effect-free;
10. operating, estate-ledger, retained-asset and claim cycles remain distinct;
11. two Fleets across two Subnets remain isolated during one co-located
    transfer;
12. 0.110 size/build/operator/validation budgets remain satisfied;
13. repository-owned qualification requires no downstream dependency; and
14. a human-requested closeout audit is accepted before another minor begins.
