# Canic 0.114 Design: Fleet Subnet Canister Estates And Reusable Reserve Fleets

Date: 2026-08-12

## Status

- Status: accepted product direction; proposed implementation design for
  maintainer review.
- Release boundary: reinstall only. A 0.114 installation does not decode,
  migrate, adopt or upgrade pool state written by an earlier Canic release.
- Implementation approval: none. No mutating batch begins until the
  [0.105 platform qualification](../0.105-fleet-estate-platform-qualification/0.105-design.md)
  is accepted and current, the 0.110 Coordinator-backed root-funding
  predecessor is accepted and complete, and the maintainer explicitly promotes
  B1.
- Sequence: this design consumes the 0.105 qualification and follows the 0.100
  root-owned physical Canister inventory, 0.101 Component provisioning and
  0.110 Coordinator-backed root funding designs. It gives the 0.113 observatory
  reservoir a generic estate authority to observe.
- Scope: one root-local reusable physical Canister estate, an ordinary
  application-neutral reserve Fleet profile, high-throughput automatic
  creation and reset, and explicit same-Subnet transfer between Fleets.
- Protocol posture: pre-1.0 hard cut. Configuration, stable state, Candid and
  CLI keep one current version-1 contract with no aliases or prior-pool
  decoder.

This document owns the future design. It does not amend the maintained 0.100
or 0.101 runtime in place and does not make their closeout depend on 0.114.

## Summary

Canic already treats an empty Canister as a paid reusable asset. The current
pool policy, however, models a small elastic `Ready` buffer: consuming one
`Ready` asset causes the local root to create another until the buffer is
restored. That behavior is unsuitable when an operator deliberately wants to
buy and retain a fixed estate of 1,000 Canisters on each selected application
Subnet.

0.114 separates three concerns:

1. the total reusable physical estate a Fleet Subnet Root should own;
2. the subset deliberately kept `Ready` and unavailable to ordinary claims;
   and
3. the bounded concurrency with which missing assets are created, reset and
   transferred.

An ordinary reserve Fleet may install one root and sibling Store on each
selected Subnet, admit no application Components, and automatically create a
fixed estate there. Its roots remain the sole physical asset authorities while
the Canisters are reserved.

An asset may later move to another Fleet, including a Fleet belonging to a
different App, only through an explicit same-Subnet transfer. The source root
first proves the asset is empty and unbound, the destination root reserves
capacity and accepts it, controller authority converges from the source root
to the destination root, and both roots retain exact terminal evidence. The
destination allocates fresh Fleet, Component and operation identity. No
application state or logical authority crosses with the physical Principal.

The operator's local ICP identity, for example an identity named
`canic-mainnet`, may authorize and sign the source and destination commands if
it controls those roots. It is not the final controller of pooled assets, is
not embedded by name or Principal in Canic, and is not a substitute for either
root's durable transfer state.

## Decision

0.114 makes these decisions:

1. Every Fleet Subnet Root owns one consolidated physical-Canister ledger.
2. The reusable estate target counts every non-infrastructure physical asset
   still owned by that root, whether pooled, claimed, installed, recycling,
   failed or represented by an unresolved creation reservation.
3. `owned_target` is distinct from `ready_reserve` and
   `owned_maximum`.
4. Consuming a `Ready` asset does not reduce owned estate and therefore does
   not automatically create a replacement merely to restore the initial
   estate.
5. Automatic creation uses several independently durable bounded lanes rather
   than one 30-second single-flight operation.
6. Creation and reset continuations are completion-driven. A timer is a
   recovery watchdog, not the throughput clock.
7. Root-local counters and indexes make hot decisions independent of complete
   inventory size.
8. A reserve Fleet is an ordinary Fleet with empty application topology and
   explicitly placed roots. It is not a fourth infrastructure role or a
   special Canister type.
9. A physical asset may transfer only between exact roots on the same physical
   Subnet.
10. Cross-Fleet transfer changes physical lifecycle authority, not App or
    Component identity. The target always allocates fresh logical identity.
11. A controller Principal may authorize disposal or import but controller
    status alone never proves Canic Fleet, root, Component or application
    authority.
12. Empty assets use a low measured standby balance and an independent
    threshold-plus-fixed-top-up step before installation. No maximum balance
    is imposed.
13. An unusually high balance is diagnostic evidence for operator review; it
    never triggers an autonomous sweep.
14. No Canic policy claims to reserve shared Subnet compute or storage. Only
    successfully created physical Canisters occupy Canister identities and
    count against the IC's Subnet Canister limit.

## Goals

0.114 must:

- create and retain estates of at least 1,000 physical Canisters per selected
  application Subnet;
- make a fixed estate and an elastic warm-spare policy independently
  expressible;
- keep exact root-local accounting across every physical asset state;
- provision as quickly as the qualified IC and Cycles Ledger boundaries
  permit, without blind paid retry or unbounded concurrency;
- let an operator pause, resume, lower effective concurrency and select a new
  target within immutable limits;
- keep paid creation and reset effects exact-retry safe in every lane;
- preserve physical Canister Principals and retained cycles when workloads are
  removed and assets are recycled;
- support an application-neutral reserve Fleet on several explicit Subnets;
- transfer exact empty assets from a reserve Fleet to any admitted destination
  Fleet and App on the same Subnet;
- support distinct source and destination operators without requiring a
  shared local identity;
- expose bounded inventory, progress, rate, ETA, funding and failure status;
- remove complete-estate scans from maintenance, claim and paged status hot
  paths;
- keep Store, root, Component and application authorities unchanged; and
- prove the 10, 100 and 1,000 asset journeys before claiming the large-estate
  envelope.

## Non-Goals

0.114 does not:

- reserve compute allocation, memory allocation or storage capacity on a
  shared IC Subnet;
- move an existing Canister Principal between physical Subnets;
- let a child or Component inspect or mutate the root's complete estate;
- create one global Canic inventory shared by several Fleets;
- let a reserve Fleet become application topology or release authority;
- preserve a prior workload's Component identity, Directory membership,
  service membership, data or Wasm during reuse;
- infer transfer authority from a caller-supplied binding or controller list;
- keep a host identity as the final controller of reserved or assigned
  assets;
- transfer a root, Coordinator or Wasm Store through the workload-estate
  protocol;
- make a cross-release adoption exception for pre-1.0 Canic state;
- automatically delete excess assets when a target is lowered;
- automatically reclaim high Canister balances to a root or Coordinator;
- mint ICP, choose an exchange rate or silently fund an estate from the root's
  operating balance;
- promise a creation duration before real throughput qualification; or
- turn Coordinator Workers into physical asset owners.

## Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| physical asset | one created Canister Principal tracked through exactly one root-owned physical lifecycle |
| estate | all reusable non-infrastructure physical assets currently owned by one Fleet Subnet Root, including work in flight |
| standby asset | an empty root-controlled asset in `PendingReset`, `Ready` or actionable `Failed` state |
| workload asset | an estate asset claimed or installed for one current Component or descendant |
| reserve Fleet | an ordinary Fleet whose explicit roots primarily retain reusable empty estates and whose application topology is empty |
| source root | current physical asset authority at the start of a transfer |
| destination root | exact same-Subnet root which reserves capacity and becomes the final asset controller |
| operator identity | external Principal authorized to invoke protected root or host workflows; a local identity name is not protocol identity |
| standby minimum | balance below which an empty asset cannot become `Ready` |
| claim threshold | balance at or below which claiming schedules one fixed top-up before installation |
| top-up amount | exact configured amount transferred when the claim threshold is met; not a target or cap |
| unresolved creation | a paid creation intent whose physical result is not yet conclusively identified |

| Concern | Sole authority |
| --- | --- |
| App identity and logical Component topology | checked-in App configuration and compiled Component Topology |
| Fleet and root identity | immutable install plan and Fleet Coordinator Registry |
| root placement | protected Fleet input and verified IC routing evidence |
| physical asset record and state | exact Fleet Subnet Root physical-Canister ledger |
| current estate target and effective concurrency | root stable admin state within immutable policy ceilings |
| immutable estate ceilings and funding policy | protected Fleet input copied into root authority |
| paid creation intent and recovery | exact root creation lane record |
| physical Canister placement | live IC routing observation |
| workload admission and logical identity | target root's compiled topology and Component Registry |
| source transfer selection | source root stable transfer batch |
| destination capacity reservation | destination root stable transfer batch |

| root operating cycles | 0.110 Coordinator/root funding authority |

The source root is authoritative about whether it may dispose of an asset. The
destination root is independently authoritative about whether it may import
and later allocate that asset. Neither root accepts the other's Component,
Fleet or App binding as local truth.

## Estate Policy

The protected Fleet input uses one current policy shape. Illustrative field
names are selected here; implementation review may improve spelling without
changing the semantics.

~~~toml
schema_version = 1

[[fleet_subnet_roots]]
placement_subnet = "<application-subnet-principal>"

[fleet_subnet_roots.canister_estate]
owned_target = 1000
owned_maximum = 1000
ready_reserve = 0
standby_minimum_cycles = "500B"
claim_threshold_cycles = "2T"
claim_top_up_cycles = "2T"
creation_parallelism = 16
reset_parallelism = 32
maximum_creation_cycles = "1100T"
creation_window_secs = 86400
overfunded_observation_cycles = "20T"
~~~

All amounts are examples, not defaults or production recommendations. The
accepted 0.105 qualification report must measure the safe standby floor, reset
cost, installation headroom and useful parallelism before configuration
guidance is accepted.

### Owned Target And Maximum

`owned_target` is the initial automatic total-estate target. The root creates
until:

~~~text
owned_estate + unresolved_reserved_slots >= current_owned_target
~~~

The count includes `PendingReset`, `Ready`, `Claimed`, `Workload`,
`Recycling`, actionable `Failed`, transfer-in-progress assets still owned by
the root and every unresolved creation slot. It excludes the Coordinator,
Fleet Subnet Root, Wasm Store, deletion-confirmed assets and terminally
transferred assets.

`owned_maximum` is an immutable root-local ceiling. A controller may change
the durable current target between zero and `owned_maximum` under a unique
operation ID. Raising it admits new creation work. Lowering it only stops
future creation; it never deletes, transfers or uninstalls an asset.

A terminal export reduces source ownership. If the source's current target is
unchanged, maintenance creates a replacement and preserves that reserve
level. An operator transferring a finite estate without replenishment first
lowers the source target under its own durable operation, then prepares the
export. This ordering makes replacement intentional rather than an accidental
side effect of transfer.

This is not a physical Subnet-wide maximum. Two Fleets on the same Subnet own
independent ledgers and cannot count or mutate one another. Children require
no knowledge of either ledger.

### Ready Reserve

`ready_reserve` is the number of `Ready` assets protected from ordinary
allocation. A claim which would reduce `Ready` below that value rejects before
mutation. It does not independently force estate growth beyond
`owned_maximum`.

For a fixed estate of 1,000 which may eventually be fully consumed:

~~~text
owned_target = 1000
owned_maximum = 1000
ready_reserve = 0
~~~

For 1,000 assignable assets plus ten permanently warm spares:

~~~text
owned_target = 1010
owned_maximum = 1010
ready_reserve = 10
~~~

The design does not overload `Ready` count as total physical capacity.

### Balance Policy

An empty asset becomes `Ready` only after exact controller, uninstall, module,
Subnet and balance observations and only when its balance is at least
`standby_minimum_cycles`.

When a claim reaches the front of installation, the root observes its exact
balance. At or below `claim_threshold_cycles`, the root transfers exactly
`claim_top_up_cycles` once under the allocation operation ID, then re-observes
the result before installation. A higher balance causes no transfer.

There is no target-balance calculation and no maximum accepted balance. An
asset above `overfunded_observation_cycles` is reported with informational
severity. Reclamation is a later explicit operator workflow because moving
cycles out of an empty Canister requires qualified code and irreversible
effects.

### Spend And Concurrency Bounds

Every creation lane reserves the current IC-native creation cost, configured
standby balance and Cycles Ledger fee before its call. Aggregate reservations,
successful spend and uncertain effects count against
`maximum_creation_cycles` within `creation_window_secs`.

Configured parallelism is a maximum, not a promise. Controller state may
pause the engine or lower effective concurrency. It may not exceed the
immutable maximum without a fresh install under the pre-1.0 rule.

Unknown fields, zero amounts, zero parallelism, `owned_target >
owned_maximum`, `ready_reserve > owned_maximum`, an impossible spend budget or
arithmetic overflow reject before any paid effect.

## Reserve Fleet Profile

A reserve Fleet is constructed from ordinary Canic infrastructure:

~~~text
one Fleet Coordinator
  + one Fleet Subnet Root per selected physical Subnet
  + one sibling Wasm Store per root
  + zero application Components
  + one root-local Canister estate policy per root
~~~

The reference profile should use an ordinary minimal App whose compiled
Component topology is empty. Fleet input explicitly names every selected root
Subnet and its estate policy. The host must support a root whose initial
Component inventory is exactly and terminally empty; it must not create a
dummy Component merely to force root placement.

The reserve Fleet gains no special role, Registry status, Candid method or
artifact. `reserve` is an operator purpose and reference configuration, not a
new authority kind. The same estate policy may be used by a normal application
Fleet.

The implementation must provide:

- one checked-in minimal reserve-App configuration;
- one documented Fleet-input template with several explicit application
  Subnets and no real principals or credentials;
- plan and dry-run output showing the exact maximum paid exposure per Subnet;
- an explicit network and identity requirement for every live creation; and
- no command which deploys or funds mainnet merely because the profile is
  selected.

The suggested operator Fleet name `canic-reserve-mainnet` is descriptive
only. Fleet identity remains the normal generated `FleetKey`, and no code may
match that display name.

## Application And Fleet Neutrality

Physical reuse deliberately separates the Canister Principal from every
logical application identity.

After transfer, the destination root records the physical origin for audit but
allocates a new:

- Fleet binding;
- root binding;
- Component or child binding;
- `ComponentInstanceId` where applicable;
- immediate parent;
- release build and role context; and
- provisioning operation identity.

The old Registry row, Directory membership, service membership, parentage,
Wasm and application data never become destination inputs. A physical
Principal may therefore serve a later workload in any App and Fleet without
making those identities interchangeable.

The transfer protocol is local to one physical Subnet. If the destination
Fleet has no root on that Subnet, the asset cannot be used there. Canic must
either select a destination root already installed on that Subnet or create a
different physical Canister on the desired Subnet through an independently
approved root-expansion design.

## Controller Model

The controller model is exact:

~~~text
reserved/ready/workload/recycling:
    [current owning Fleet Subnet Root]

bounded transfer window:
    [source Fleet Subnet Root, destination Fleet Subnet Root]

terminal destination ownership:
    [destination Fleet Subnet Root]
~~~

The operator identity is normally a controller of the infrastructure roots
and may invoke their controller-only administration endpoints. It is not added
to every asset. In particular:

- the local identity name `canic-mainnet` is a host alias, not an IC-native
  role;
- its resolved Principal may differ between machines or operators;
- possession of that key grants only the controller operations configured for
  the exact root;
- it does not prove Fleet, root, Component, App or same-Subnet membership; and
- loss or rotation of that key must not invalidate already committed asset
  ownership.

When source and destination Fleets have different operators, each operator
approves its own root's durable half of the same transfer plan. No shared
private key or permanent common asset controller is required.

## Cross-Fleet Transfer Protocol

Transfer is a two-authority saga. A host plan may coordinate it, but root
records and live IC observations are the recovery authorities.

### Eligibility

Before selection, the source root requires:

- root is `Active`, or is `Draining` under the exact terminal-disposition
  fence, and no root deletion handoff conflicts;
- the asset is `Ready`, has no Component claim and has no other transfer;
- exact live routing still resolves it to the source root's Subnet;
- exact controllers are `[source_root]`;
- no Wasm module is installed;
- observed balance satisfies standby policy; and
- target root Principal is non-reserved and distinct.

Before reservation, the destination root requires:

- its own lifecycle is `Active` and permits import and allocation;
- immutable estate policy admits the complete requested batch;
- the asset is not already present in its physical ledger or Registry;
- destination root placement equals the source asset's live Subnet;
- the exact transfer operation is controller-approved; and
- no other import reserves the same Principal or capacity.

The host resolves both roots from their independent terminal Coordinator
catalogs, checks the physical Subnet match and writes a bounded no-follow plan
with one operation ID and canonical asset list. That host document aids
operator recovery but never overrides either root's state.

### State Machines

The source owns:

~~~text
Ready
  -> ExportPrepared
  -> DestinationReserved
  -> CoControllerIntent
  -> CoControlled
  -> DestinationAccepted
  -> SourceReleaseIntent
  -> Transferred
~~~

The destination owns:

~~~text
ImportPrepared
  -> SourcePrepared
  -> CoControlledObserved
  -> ResetVerified
  -> SoleControllerIntent
  -> SourceReleasedObserved
  -> Ready
~~~

The ordered journey is:

1. destination persists capacity and exact-principal reservations;
2. source persists the canonical export batch and destination receipt;
3. source persists controller-effect intent;
4. source changes controllers to exactly `[source_root, destination_root]`;
5. both roots independently observe that exact set;
6. destination uninstalls any code again, observes no module, balance and
   routing, and commits its acceptance receipt;
7. source retains that exact receipt and persists release intent;
8. either qualified co-controller converges controllers to exactly
   `[destination_root]` under the same forward-only operation;
9. destination re-observes sole control and atomically converts its reserved
   import into `Ready` inventory; and
10. source re-observes loss of control and commits a permanent transfer
    tombstone.

Once co-control is observed, cancellation is prohibited. Recovery converges
forward to the admitted destination because either root can establish the
final controller set. Before co-control, exact cancellation may release both
reservations only after proving no controller effect occurred.

Every await has durable intent before it. Response loss causes exact status
observation or exact retry, never selection of another asset or destination.
Transport failure, rejected status, stale routing and unreachability are not
physical absence or successful transfer.

### Batch Transfer

A batch freezes at most the configured bounded number of canonical Principals
before effects. Each asset has an independent lane and terminal receipt while
the batch owns aggregate reservation and progress. One failed lane does not
hold the stable lock of another lane, but terminal batch success requires
every selected asset or an explicit pre-effect cancellation.

The source may select oldest `Ready` assets or an exact operator-supplied
subset. Selection is persisted once. A retry cannot substitute a cheaper,
newer or differently funded asset.

## High-Throughput Maintenance Engine

The current one-operation maintenance shape is replaced by bounded durable
lanes.

### Creation Lanes

For every admitted lane, the root persists before the Cycles Ledger call:

- never-reused lane and creation sequence;
- operation ID;
- exact root and placement Subnet;
- exact Cycles Ledger;
- strictly increasing `created_at_time`;
- retained balance, runtime creation cost, fee and total amount;
- estate slot and aggregate spend reservation;
- attempt and uncertainty state; and
- immutable policy hash.

The Cycles Ledger request fixes exact Subnet and root controller. Success and
`Duplicate` with the same returned Principal converge on one asset. `TooOld`
after an uncertain result remains blocked and continues occupying its estate
slot and spend reservation; the engine cannot create a replacement that might
later exceed `owned_maximum`.

Independent lanes may be in flight together. No call allocates more than one
Canister, and no one update attempts to open an unbounded number of calls or
callbacks.

### Reset Lanes

Created, imported and recycled assets enter a stable pending-reset index.
Reset lanes independently:

1. persist exact asset and expected prior state;
2. establish the owning root as the exact permitted controller set;
3. uninstall code;
4. observe no module, live Subnet and cycles;
5. update balance classification; and
6. commit `Ready` or actionable `Failed` state.

Creation completion schedules reset immediately. Reset completion schedules
another pending item immediately. A bounded watchdog resumes abandoned lanes
after restart; it does not delay healthy throughput.

### Adaptive Scheduling

While below target, lane completion schedules zero-delay continuation until
the concurrency window is full or no work remains. While healthy, maintenance
sleeps and wakes only for:

- a claim or recycle transition;
- a target/concurrency/pause command;
- a funding or retry event;
- an actionable failed asset; or
- a low-frequency reconciliation deadline.

This removes continuous full-estate polling and makes a healthy 1,000-asset
estate inexpensive to retain.

## Stable Index And Counter Contract

The physical asset record remains the normalized truth. Secondary structures
must include:

- exact counters for every physical state and origin class;
- total owned estate and unresolved slot counts;
- a `(ready_since_ns, canister_id)` index;
- a pending-reset index;
- a failed/actionable index;
- an allocation-operation-to-Canister claim index;
- incoming and outgoing transfer indexes; and
- creation lane and operation indexes.

Claim, reset, transfer and status hot paths may not call a complete inventory
`export()` merely to count, select one item or return one page. Status paging
iterates the primary stable map from an opaque cursor and reads at most the
bounded examined-row limit.

Every transition validates the old primary record, relevant index entry,
counter deltas and checked encoded-byte delta before mutation. The primary
record, indexes, counters and journal settle in the same replicated message.
Mismatch after restart fails closed and identifies the exact structure; it is
not silently repaired from whichever copy is convenient.

The implementation inventory must allocate exact stable-memory IDs and maxima
inside the control-plane range before coding. It must not reuse application
IDs or revive a broad Subnet Registry authority.

## Funding Boundary

Creating an estate consumes the root Principal's exact default Cycles Ledger
account. This balance is distinct from:

- the root's operating cycles balance;
- the Coordinator's operating treasury;
- any root-held ICP Ledger balance; and
- cycles already retained by estate assets.

The initial 0.114 journey assumes the operator pre-funds each root's Cycles
Ledger account with the complete approved estate budget. The host dry run and
status report the exact account, required amount, available amount and maximum
exposure. Mainnet creation never begins from a merely estimated or implicitly
selected account.

0.110 may keep the root operational while the estate engine runs. It does not
silently route Coordinator grants into the pool account. A later automatic
treasury-to-pool deposit requires its own durable value-transfer journal and
explicit 0.110/0.114 amendment.

The exact creation request amount per new asset is:

~~~text
standby_minimum_cycles
  + live ic0.cost_create_canister()
~~~

The root independently freezes that request amount and separately accounts
for the exact Cycles Ledger fee, so total debited exposure is request plus
fee. A host estimate is operator information, not runtime authority.

## Cost And Capacity Truth

The operator surface separates:

- creation cycles consumed permanently;
- Cycles Ledger fees consumed;
- cycles retained in empty assets;
- cycles reserved by unresolved operations;
- root execution and management-call burn;
- current installed storage and compute allocation; and
- informational ICP/XDR conversion at an explicitly dated rate.

Retained asset balances are committed IC fuel, not recurring rent and not
convertible back into ICP. They remain available to the later workload or an
explicit qualified reclamation journey.

Successfully creating 1,000 empty Canisters occupies 1,000 Canister identities
on the selected Subnet. It does not guarantee future compute scheduling,
memory allocation or storage headroom and cannot exclude third-party use of
the shared Subnet. The preflight reports current trusted Subnet evidence and
the IC rejects real exhaustion. Canic does not fabricate a stronger
reservation claim.

## Operator And CLI Surface

Exact command names remain an implementation review gate. The maintained
shape must support the following capabilities in ASCII lexicographic order:

~~~text
canic pool inspect <fleet> <root>
canic pool maintain <fleet> <root>
canic pool pause <fleet> <root>
canic pool resume <fleet> <root>
canic pool set-concurrency <fleet> <root> <count>
canic pool set-target <fleet> <root> <count>
canic pool transfer plan ...
canic pool transfer run <plan>
canic pool transfer status <plan>
~~~

Every mutation resolves Fleet and root from the terminal Coordinator catalog,
requires an explicit network and exact root for multi-root Fleets, and carries
a durable operation ID. A target or concurrency reduction never destroys
assets. A resume command reconciles old lanes before opening new work.

Text and JSON status include bounded:

- immutable and current targets;
- immutable and effective concurrency;
- pause and draining fences;
- counts by exact asset state;
- unresolved slot and spend reservations;
- available and required Cycles Ledger balance;
- completed/failed creation and reset rate;
- observation time and explicitly labelled ETA estimate;
- pending transfer batches and principals; and
- next cursor.

An unreachable or contradictory root exits nonzero rather than emitting a
fabricated partial total. Fleet-wide display may aggregate exact root summaries
but cannot become estate authority.

## Lifecycle Rules

### Fresh Install

The host persists Fleet, root, Store, topology, estate and funding authority
before effects. A reserve root reaches the ordinary `Prepared` and `Active`
barriers with an exactly empty Component inventory. Estate creation starts
only after root activation and explicit funding readiness.

Fleet activation does not wait for all 1,000 optional reserve assets unless
the operator explicitly selects a blocking initial-estate policy. The default
activates infrastructure first and reports estate progress independently.

### Same-Release Restart

Lifecycle restoration synchronously validates lane, index, counter, transfer
and funding reservations and then schedules continuation. No lifecycle hook
awaits. Existing nonterminal work resumes before a new lane is allocated.

### Workload Removal

Ordinary Component removal preserves the existing post-order application
fences, removes logical membership and resets the physical Canister back to
`Ready`. Total estate ownership never decrements during this state transfer.

### Root Draining

Draining stops new creation, target growth, claims and imports. Existing
creation results are reconciled and current transfers converge. The draining
root may still prepare an operator-approved export as a terminal-disposition
operation after its workload and allocation fences are closed. Every retained
asset is then either:

- handed to one exact admitted same-Subnet destination root;
- retained until an explicit operator disposition exists; or
- processed by the separately qualified physical deletion path.

Root final inventory cannot claim empty while an estate asset, unresolved
lane or transfer reservation remains.

The maintained external-recipient handoff remains a break-glass terminal root
disposition outside managed cross-Fleet reuse. An asset controlled by an
external identity is not `Ready` in any Fleet and cannot be claimed until an
exact destination-root import completes. The ordinary reusable path ends with
the destination root as sole controller.

### Pre-1.0 Release Transition

0.114 does not make prior root state adoptable by a newly installed release.
Application workloads may be reinstalled and physical assets may move between
Fleets running the exact maintained 0.114 transfer contract. Preserving an
estate while replacing its owning root across incompatible Canic releases
requires an explicit future exception, or a complete same-contract transfer
to a successor root before the old root is removed.

## Failure And Interruption Safety

Every paid or controller-changing lane is tested at:

1. immediately before intent persistence;
2. after intent and before invocation;
3. after the external effect with response lost;
4. after response and before local commit;
5. after local commit and before caller receipt;
6. same-release restart and exact retry; and
7. contradictory observed state.

Required failure semantics include:

- insufficient ledger balance pauses new creation without discarding intent;
- definite creation rejection releases only the exact lane reservation;
- unknown creation remains charged against capacity and budget;
- duplicate operation identity with a different asset, root, Subnet, amount or
  target rejects;
- one physical Principal cannot occupy two root ledgers;
- a failed reset remains actionable and cannot be claimed;
- lowering concurrency never abandons already in-flight lanes;
- transfer cannot select a different destination after co-control;
- physical absence, wrong Subnet, wrong module and wrong controller set fail
  closed;
- one failed lane does not retain another lane's in-memory lock;
- no target can claim an imported asset before sole control and final reset;
  and
- no source can reissue a terminally transferred Principal.

## Security Requirements

0.114 must prove:

- a foreign Fleet cannot list, claim, transfer or count another root's assets;
- a foreign co-located root gains nothing from same-Subnet placement alone;
- a Component or child cannot invoke estate administration;
- caller-supplied Fleet, App, Component or controller facts never become
  authority;
- a host identity which controls a root does not become asset controller by
  implication;
- an operator cannot transfer root, Coordinator or Store infrastructure
  through workload commands;
- source and destination Subnet observations are exact and current before
  controller mutation;
- controller status alone cannot activate target Registry membership;
- transfer erases source application state before target admission;
- transfer operation and asset lists are bounded and canonical;
- concurrency and spend reservations use checked arithmetic;
- no ambiguous, wildcard or omitted root selection reaches a paid effect;
- mainnet requires explicit network, identity and cost confirmation; and
- secrets, passwords and identity aliases never enter plans, stable state,
  metrics or receipts.

## Stable State, Candid And Layering

0.114 is a reinstall-only hard cut of the current pool schema. It keeps one
normalized physical asset record and adds exact role-owned lane, counter,
index, admin and transfer records. There is no compatibility decoder or
migration reader.

Public boundary types are passive version-1 DTOs. Required endpoint families
are limited to:

- controller estate policy/admin updates;
- bounded estate status queries;
- destination import preparation and status;
- source export preparation and status; and
- exact root-to-root transfer acknowledgement.

Endpoint authentication occurs before the first mutation. Cross-root updates
bind caller, operation, source, destination, Subnet, asset, expected state and
controller observation. Generated Candid and clients advance atomically and
contain no superseded pool fields.

Layer ownership remains:

- `model`: authoritative physical records, counters, indexes and invariants;
- `ops`: deterministic transitions and single management/Ledger effects;
- `policy`: pure target, reservation, concurrency, spend and eligibility
  decisions;
- `workflow`: creation/reset scheduling and transfer sagas;
- `endpoints`: authentication, decoding and immediate delegation;
- `canic-host`: strict Fleet input, reserve profile, catalog resolution,
  immutable plans and dry-run cost evidence; and
- `canic-cli`: operator orchestration, signing and bounded rendering.

No host planner enters Wasm and no Component contains root estate machinery.

## Relationship To Other Designs

### 0.100

0.100 supplies the consolidated root-owned physical inventory, exact Subnet
placement, prepaid claim/recycle behavior and one-at-a-time draining handoff.
0.114 hard-cuts its buffer-oriented target semantics and single-flight scans
for the new estate model. It does not weaken 0.100 Component or root authority.

### 0.101

0.101 Components and Groups consume one exact `Ready` asset per physical
member. Their admission and logical limits remain authoritative. A reserve
Fleet has no Component Groups or Fleet services.

### 0.102

Implemented 0.114 failures use the maintained compact diagnostic catalog. The
0.102 line does not need to predict future codes and gains no pool authority.

### 0.105

0.105 owns the evidence-only empty-root, Cycles Ledger, throughput, balance,
controller/routing and current-state qualification. 0.114 consumes its accepted
report and revalidates only facts made stale by a changed platform contract or
environment. It does not reproduce qualification work as a mutating batch.

### 0.106 And 0.107

Estate transfer is a lifecycle protocol, not application data transport.
Coordinator Workers may later partition observation for very large root
counts, but roots retain asset state and paid effects.

### 0.108

Authentication profiles may improve operator login. They do not replace
controller authorization, transfer intent or root-local resource authority.

### 0.110

0.110 funds the root's operating balance. 0.114 spends a separately visible
root Cycles Ledger account on physical creation. Neither balance silently
funds the other.

### 0.113

The observatory may display estate and transfer status as evidence-labelled
views. It never starts creation or transfer from a public page and never
becomes an asset catalog.

## Validation Matrix

### Pure And Stable

- target, maximum and reserve boundary equality;
- all physical states counted exactly once;
- unresolved slot and spend accounting;
- canonical oldest-Ready selection;
- claim retry returns one Principal;
- counter/index mismatch fails closed;
- target decrease causes no lifecycle effect;
- concurrency decrease preserves in-flight work; and
- cursor examines and returns bounded rows.

### Creation And Reset

- N admitted lanes produce N distinct operation IDs and timestamps;
- duplicate receipt adopts one exact Principal;
- unknown `TooOld` result never opens a replacement slot;
- independent definite failure releases only its lane;
- restart resumes every lane without duplicate spending;
- actual Subnet and controller set are re-observed;
- reset failure remains actionable; and
- target completion puts the scheduler into quiescent mode.

### Transfer

- same Fleet and different Fleet transfers use the same physical protocol;
- different Apps receive fresh logical identity;
- different root operator identities can approve independently;
- wrong Subnet rejects before controller mutation;
- wrong source/destination/asset/operation rejects;
- source response loss after co-control converges forward;
- destination response loss after reset converges forward;
- neither source nor destination can double-count the Principal;
- target cannot claim before sole-controller observation;
- source cannot reuse after terminal tombstone; and
- Store/root/Coordinator Principals reject.

### Scale And Cost

- 10, 100 and 1,000 asset target convergence;
- measured creation/reset rate and lane saturation;
- root instruction and stable-access cost while filling and quiescent;
- exact creation fee, ledger fee, retained balance and reservation totals;
- one slow lane does not serialize unrelated lanes;
- pagination remains bounded at 1,000 and the configured future ceiling; and
- mainnet evidence is explicitly network-, identity-, date- and Subnet-bound.

## Completion Criteria

0.114 is complete only when the accepted 0.105 qualification remains current
or its affected live measurements have been narrowly revalidated, and:

1. fixed estate, warm reserve and immutable maximum are independent;
2. a consumed workload asset remains counted in the estate;
3. a reserve Fleet with zero Components installs explicit roots on several
   Subnets without a dummy role;
4. each root can autonomously create at least 1,000 exact local assets from a
   pre-funded Cycles Ledger account;
5. the qualified concurrent engine has durable intent and exact retry per
   lane;
6. target convergence is completion-driven rather than 30-second serialized;
7. maintenance, claim and status hot paths perform no complete-estate scan;
8. pause, resume, target and effective concurrency controls remain within
   immutable ceilings;
9. standby minimum and claim fixed top-up are independent and no balance cap
   exists;
10. high balances are observable but never autonomously swept;
11. same-Subnet assets transfer between different Fleets and Apps without
    changing their Principal;
12. transfer removes all source application authority and allocates fresh
    destination logical identity;
13. exact asset controllers converge from source root, through bounded
    co-control, to destination root only;
14. a local operator identity may authorize but never becomes implicit asset
    authority;
15. source and destination roots may have different external operators;
16. wrong Subnet, controller, module, Principal or retry payload fails closed;
17. operating, pool-ledger, retained and consumed cycles remain distinct;
18. 10, 100 and 1,000 journeys publish exact measured duration and cost;
19. current Candid, generated clients, configuration, CLI, docs and stable
    allocations contain no superseded pool contract; and
20. the closeout audit finds no cross-Fleet shared authority, unbounded scan,
    blind paid retry, implicit mainnet effect or prior-release decoder.

## Open Implementation Gates

The accepted 0.105 report must already freeze measured creation/reset
parallelism, standby and claim-funding recommendations, the current stable-ID
and scan inventory, and the smallest safe mainnet qualification. 0.114 must
still freeze:

1. exact future stable-memory IDs and encoded maxima;
2. maximum lanes and assets per transfer batch;
3. the root-to-root acknowledgement proof shape;
4. whether the host transfer plan is JSON or another current canonical
   document format;
5. exact CLI noun ordering within the maintained command inventory; and
6. whether automatic funding of the root Cycles Ledger account receives a
   separately approved extension after the pre-funded v1 journey.

Those gates may tighten limits and names. They may not change sole root
physical authority, fixed-estate accounting, intent-before-effect, same-Subnet
transfer, fresh destination logical identity or the exclusion of local
operator identities from final asset controller sets without a
maintainer-approved design amendment.
